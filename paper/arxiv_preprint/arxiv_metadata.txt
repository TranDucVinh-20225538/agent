Title:
When the World Changes but the Score Does Not: A Counterfactual Audit of Computer-Use Agents

Authors:
Vinh Duc Tran (Hanoi University of Science and Technology) <tranducvinh2004@gmail.com>

Primary category:
cs.AI

Cross-list:
cs.LG

Comments:
Preprint. A version is under review at the Evaluation of Interactive Agents workshop at NeurIPS 2026 (non-archival).

Abstract:
Computer-use agents are typically scored by whether an episode finishes and what a rubric judge assigns. That scalar mixes three questions: termination, whether the final answer reflected the world the agent saw, and whether the judge noticed when that world changed. We separate them with paired counterfactual episodes on MyPCBench: a locked intervention rewrites a pre-registered determining set D while instruction, interface, and judge stay fixed. Tracking is read from the final answer against guest gold, never from the score; score sensitivity is exact equality S_CF = S_Base versus any nonzero delta. Across agents, successful counterfactual tracking sometimes leaves the benchmark score unchanged and sometimes changes it; conversely, unchanged high scores can occur despite incomplete state tracking. On a frozen 5x10 design (50 intended cells; 4 never scheduled; 46 executed; 92 legs; 35 execution failures; 24 valid pairs), Claude has 9 valid pairs (7 tracking-valid: 6 Type A, 1 score-sensitive, 2 Type B; invariance 6/7=0.857, Clopper-Pearson 95% CI [0.421, 0.996]); GPT-5.5 has 8 valid (7 tracking-valid: 3 Type A, 4 score-sensitive, 1 Type B; 3/7=0.429, [0.099, 0.816]); Qwen3.5-35B-A3B has one valid pair (Type A; [0.025, 1.000]). Qwen3.5-9B (ablation) and Qwen3.8-Flash (exploratory) are not pooled into these rates. A stable score does not certify tracking; a moving score does not certify a tracking failure.
